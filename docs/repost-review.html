Kimi K3 on 8 GB RAM with no GPU: a 2.78-trillion-parameter open-weight LLM ran locally, and the real hardware requirement is your disk, not your memory.
Free setup guide: https://withhammad.com/resources/kimi-k3-on-8gb-ram

How kimi-k3-in-c, an Apache-2.0 C engine by Fareed Khan, does it:
- About 93% of Moonshot AI's 1.56 TB checkpoint is mixture-of-experts weights.
- Those experts stay on a fast NVMe SSD, and each token reads only 16 of 896 experts per layer.
- Output is byte-identical from 8 GB to 224 GB of RAM. More memory changes the speed, not the answer.

What it really costs:
- About 1.7 TB of free disk before you download a single byte.
- Hours of downloading (about 3.5 hours at 1 Gbps).
- About 30 seconds per token, measured on a 124-core server with its RAM capped at 8 GB.
- On an 8 GB Jetson board with a hard drive, one word ("Paris") took nearly 16 minutes.

So treat it as a proof of concept, not an everyday app: text only, no server, no quality benchmark.

For UAE businesses that handle client data, the interesting part is privacy. A model you run locally keeps prompts on your own machine; a hosted API sends them to another company's servers. The memory wall did not disappear. It moved to your disk.

Kimi K3 is open weights under Moonshot's own Kimi K3 License, a custom license rather than an OSI open-source one. The engine is Apache-2.0.
