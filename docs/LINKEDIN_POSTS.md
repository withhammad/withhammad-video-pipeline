# LinkedIn posts: the 10-3-1 method (owner rule, 14 Nov 2026)

The owner asked for every LinkedIn post to be written with the method Lara Acosta explains in her reel
(instagram.com/reel/DfsSCn-xiwG, "Going viral is easy if you master this one copywriting method"). It is her
method, credited to her; we apply it, we don't present it as ours.

## The method

| Part | Rule | Why (her words, paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | No line longer than 10 words. One idea per line, a blank line between short blocks. | People read on their phone. A long sentence on a phone looks like homework or an essay; short lines look easy, so they keep reading. |
| **3** | Use the rule of three with emotional words. Not "he was having a hard time" but "he was stressed, broken and burnt out." | Three feeling words land; readers feel them. |
| **1** | End the body on one punchy line that sums up the whole post. | Like the end of a speech. It's the line people quote back in the comments. |

## How it fits our other LinkedIn rules

These rules stay as they are:
- The first ~210 characters (before "see more") hold the keyword hook.
- The guide link and a comment question.
- The AI disclosure line.
- 0-3 hashtags at the very end.

Order of a post:
1. **Hook:** 1-2 lines, each ≤ 10 words, with the topic keyword. Must work on its own before "see more".
2. **Body:** short lines (≤ 10 words), short blocks. At least one rule-of-three line with emotional words. Never use invented feelings or facts; the three words must be true to the story.
3. **The one line:** a single short sentence that sums up the post. It closes the body.
4. **CTA block,** after a blank line:
   - the guide link;
   - one question that invites comments;
   - the AI disclosure line;
   - 0-3 hashtags.

## Checklist (the seo-pack verify step runs it)

- [ ] Every body line ≤ 10 words. URLs, the disclosure and the hashtag line are exempt.
- [ ] At least one rule-of-three line, with emotional or sensory words.
- [ ] The body ends on one punchy line of ≤ 12 words. The CTA block comes after it.
- [ ] The keyword is in the first 210 characters.
- [ ] Guide link, question, disclosure and 0-3 hashtags are present.

## Example: the 14 Nov "video edit prompt" post, rewritten

Before: long paragraphs, e.g. "Claude Code won't edit a video out of the box. Ask it to 'edit this video' and you get a plan, not an MP4."

After:

```
Claude Code won't edit your video.
Not out of the box.

Ask it to "edit this video."
You get a plan. Not an MP4.

I was stuck, annoyed and close to giving up.

Then I gave it 7 free installs.
And one copy-paste prompt.

Now it cuts, captions and exports.
Every video. Same rules. Fewer notes.

The prompt is the editor. Claude just follows it.

Copy-paste prompt (free): https://withhammad.com/...
Would you let Claude Code cut your next video?
The phones in the video play reels with an AI avatar of me.
#ClaudeCode #VideoEditing #AI
```

The example's feelings ("stuck, annoyed and close to giving up") must match what really happened. If they didn't,
use three true words instead.
